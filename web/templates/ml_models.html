what xwin and xedge actually listen to
rating pass→xleadxfrontxgal→xodds→xwinxedge

Purpose. How strong every horse, driver and stable is, comparable across every horse they have met, directly or through others. Every layer above reads it: xlead uses it to weigh a horse against its field, and xodds and xwin start from it. There are two strengths, because the market's opinion and the race results are different evidence.

The price rating: only win-pool prices. Each priced Nordic race since 2012 (SE, NO, DK, FI; five or more starters, all with odds) nudges the horse, driver and stable ratings so that together with post, tillägg and track effects they explain the closing shares. A race's surprise is split by uncertainty, so a seasoned driver (prior variance 0.03) or stable (0.02) moves far less than a horse (0.3). A horse's uncertainty grows with every day it isn't priced (×2 at age 3 or younger, ×1.5 at 4) and stops growing at 0.8. A horse never priced enters at the average newcomer's first rating.

The results rating: whom it beat. Every race with a result since 2012, in every country stored, read as a ranking: the winner beat the whole field, second beat the rest, third the rest after that (second and third count 0.7 and 0.49 of the winner; lower places are mostly noise once a driver eases up). Galloped, disqualified and unplaced runners lose to every placed one. France is stored with full fields, so a horse raced there is placed against the Nordic horses that cross over. The scale is the same log win odds as the price rating, with its own driver and post effects and an uncertainty that grows with days off. A loss counts in full even when the comment gives an excuse (start trouble, death seat, wide, no room): discounting it was tested and fit worse.

What it hands on beside the two strengths: form against its odds (pairwise against every rival, 120-day half-life) and the last race against its odds, margins behind the winner, km-time records against this field, galopp rate, shoes and sulky, earnings, days off, the stable's and driver's recent form, and the last comment's trip (death seat, wide, disturbed, start trouble, finish, strength). These don't move the rating; xodds and xwin weigh them.

What it doesn't read:

  • Prices abroad. The price rating only sees Nordic win pools; a horse from France reaches it through the results rating alone.
  • xLabs sectional times (first 200, last 200, best 100). The horse-card ratings (Elo and sub-ratings) use them; no layer of this chain does yet.
  • Older comments (only the last one is kept) and pedigree.

The card's score is the price rating's own shares (horse + driver + stable + post, softmaxed per race) on the forward test, as a model in its own right. The results rating is scored the same way beside it, and by what it adds to the market and to the price rating.

diagnostics · forward test
models

A rough estimate, on purpose. Each layer's out-of-sample predictions (the forward test from July 2025, then every live prediction since the build) are played with fixed rules and settled against what ATG actually paid. Our own stake is added to the pool, so a hit dilutes itself. Place uses the top-2/3 chance from the win probabilities (Harville) and the board's minimum place odds. Marking games use the real stake shares and per-tier payouts; V6/V7 rows count 2–2.5 units in the top tier.

Read the interval, not the number. One big V64 or V86 hit can carry a year. Hover any column header (halves, market, 90%, …) for what it means. The halves and the ROI without the biggest return show whether a result holds up; market and public play the same rule on the same games, so every model row has a like-for-like reference.

rating pass→xleadxfrontxgal→xodds→xwinxedge

Purpose: what the market will think. The win odds a starter should carry, given everything public before the race, shown in the race list as payout ÷ share. It is fitted to the win pool's shares, not to results, so by design it copies the market, habits and over-reactions included. It isn't built to beat the market; its job is to give xwin a market baseline before any odds exist, so xwin can spend its effort on where results disagree.

How. It starts from the rating pass (see the rating tab) and adds everything the pass hands on: form against the odds, the last race against its odds, km-time records against this field, margins, galopp rate, the last comment's trip, shoes and sulky, days off, the stable's and driver's form, and the starter's xlead share. A multinomial logit plus residual trees is fitted to the market's shares in Swedish races.

Opponent comparisons live in the rating pass, and they are made of prices: A priced over C and C over B puts A over B without the two ever meeting. Results against opponents only come in as form against the odds, pairwise against every rival in the race relative to their prices. The card's input groups show how much each part moves the fit.

Gap closed is how far xodds gets from equal chances toward the market's own prices, tested forward on races the models never saw.

models
rating pass→xleadxfrontxgal→xodds→xwinxedge

Purpose: who wins the start. The chance a starter is in front after the first 100–150 m. There is one model per start method (auto, volt), on Swedish tracks only, with the track as an input. A horse leading early wins ~27% of races. The lead changes hands by 700 m in ~39% of auto races (~31% of volt races), and an early leader that gives it up wins only ~6%. Its partner xfront asks who is in front once that has settled, at 700 m.

Label. It comes from the race comment's opening (Ledn, Tog ledn, Släppte ledn e 400). A race only teaches when exactly one starter reads as the early leader and at least 80% of the field has a comment, so a missing comment can't hide the real leader.

Inputs, all as of race morning:

  • how often each post leads, overall and on this track
  • the horse's and the driver's lead rate against what their posts promised
  • early position, fast starts and start trouble from past comments
  • a driver change, and the early speed of everyone inside and outside it
  • how strongly the rating pass prices the horse and its driver against this field (the best horse and the top driver often get to choose)

Shares are normalised so the field sums to 100%. The comparison is against the post baseline: how often that post leads on that track. Faded cards are earlier versions tested from January 2025; for a like-for-like comparison, use the live card's first version row, which ran on the same races.

models
rating pass→xleadxfrontxgal→xodds→xwinxedge

Purpose: who wins, before any odds exist. xodds learns what the market will think; xwin starts from the xodds share and learns where results part ways with it. Its inputs are where xodds and the results rating put the horse in its field, xlead, xfront, xgal, the race shape (post, tillägg, distance), and every feature of the rating pass again.

Trained race by race. The trees (LightGBM) are grown on the winner's likelihood within its race, so the field's chances sum to one while it learns, instead of a yes/no per runner normalised afterwards. They start from the log xodds share, so every tree is a correction to it.

The inputs aren't weighted equally, or by design. The card's input groups show how much winner log-loss each part adds when it's shuffled. The xodds starting point itself is never shuffled, so the groups split what the trees add on top of it; the earlier classifier had to relearn xodds and gave everything to it.

Stacking is only honest out of fold. Every xodds, xlead, xfront or xgal value xwin trained on came from a model that never saw that race: four time blocks from 2021 to mid-2025, each predicted by models fitted on the others. The forward test runs the whole chain on models fitted before it. Scoring an upcoming race is out of sample by nature.

The yardstick is winner log-loss: uniform, xodds and the market's own prices against what happened. Gap closed is how far from uniform toward the market xwin gets, without ever seeing a price for the race itself.

models
rating pass→xleadxfrontxgal→xodds→xwinxedge

Purpose: who is in front once the start has settled. The chance a starter is in front at 700 m. By then the lead has changed hands in ~39% of auto races and ~31% of volt races, and the 700 m leader wins ~41% / ~39% of the time. Like xlead there is one model per start method, on Swedish tracks, with the same inputs; only the label differs.

Label. The race comment read as a timeline: each clause with a distance (ledn e 400, 1450 kv, varvet kv) is placed in the race, an undated first clause opens it. The starter in front at 700 m is the one whose last takeover before 700 m still holds. Two claims resolve to the later takeover; a race teaches only when that leaves exactly one leader and at least 80% of the field has a comment.

What it adds. Its out-of-fold shares feed xwin and xedge. In front of the market it carried about twice what xlead does.

models
rating pass→xleadxfrontxgal→xodds→xwinxedge

Purpose: where the odds are wrong. The same inputs and the same race-by-race trees as xwin, but they start from the market's own shares (the closing win odds). All they can learn is a correction: the market's favourite–longshot bias, and where form, trip, gallop risk and the lead signals say the public has a horse wrong. It is the probability the pools tab's xedge rows and the coupon agent play.

A tilt, not a price. The trees never see the race's own odds, so a starter's correction is fixed before any money is in: it's computed nightly with the other models and multiplied into whatever odds are on the board, then renormalised over the field. It learned on closing odds; earlier odds are thinner, so it's best read close to the start.

Evidence. The card's per-block table: every out-of-fold block from 2021 to mid-2025 and the forward test from July 2025, against the market on the same races. The recalibrated market row is the market alone with its bias removed (its log shares times one fitted power); the gap between it and xedge is what the inputs add beyond the bias.

models

Purpose: how to play a marking game. For every V4 … V86 game the agent sees a menu of coupons built from xedge's chances: 0–3 bankers by four rules, five coverages, flat rows or weighted rows (weight ∝ chance, or ∝ chance² ÷ the public's share, whole or half units), the top pool where there is one, each played ×1 to ×10. It plays the coupon it scores highest, or passes.

What it looks at. Before the race, each coupon's expected return under xedge's chances and under the market's alone (the public's winning rows estimated from its stake shares), its cost against the bankroll, its chance of paying anything, its shape and the game type. The score is linear in those; its weights are the policy.

Two policies. Days before a game the win pools are empty, so there are no odds for xedge to correct. Until every leg's win pool holds money the agent plays a preliminary policy on xwin's chances against xodds (the forecast of the odds); after that the live one on xedge against the odds. Each is fitted on its own inputs: without the market the forecasts expect several times what their coupons return, and the live policy fed them overbets.

How it learned. The cross-entropy method on the games before the test period: the weights that grow a 10,000 kr bankroll fastest (log growth), with each policy's three luckiest games left out so it can't be chosen for jackpots it happened to catch. Then frozen and played forward over every later game on out-of-sample chances, settled against what ATG paid with our stake diluting the pool. Read the interval and the result without the biggest win, not the headline. The backtest knows each leg's closing odds and the pool's final stake shares; on the day, later legs' odds are still forming when the game closes, so expect less.

The game page's build sphere loads the coupon it would play on that game into the builder, as a starting point. It is never handed in; picks still where the agent put them show in black ink (white at night). A race-day size switch there sizes a days-ahead coupon with the live policy, the "live policy on the forecasts" row above.
